# H16.1d: more area rows (OpenStreetMap) and more district headquarters (Wikipedia)

Data only, in the same CSV formats, a source address on every new row. Follow-up to H16.1a (Opus point 2). No hand-made lists, no invented numbers.

## What was added
- `india-areas.csv`: +238 rows for 9 more big cities (Jaipur 40, Nagpur 40, Nashik 40, Faridabad 40, Ghaziabad 29, Ludhiana 16, Aurangabad 15, Vadodara 11, Varanasi 10). The file now has 802 areas for 28 cities. Each row is a place that OpenStreetMap tags `place=suburb`, `quarter` or `neighbourhood` inside the municipal boundary of the city (the boundary that contains the city's centre point, administrative level 7 to 10); the source is the address of that OpenStreetMap object. Ranking when a city has more than 40: objects with a Wikidata or Wikipedia tag first, then suburb before quarter before neighbourhood, then by name. Names that are not plain Latin names, names that are a block letter ("C Block"), a housing society, a tower, a village or have a comma were dropped.
- `india-cities.csv`: +9 district headquarters (Likhabali, Niuland, Shamator, Quepem, Hansi, Mohla, Raga, Meluri, Tamulpur). Each is the "seat" or "headquarters" line of the infobox on the district's Wikipedia page, and that page is the source. The file now has 895 rows.

## What could not be added (nothing invented)
- Areas: Meerut (5 OpenStreetMap objects) and Rajkot (6) have fewer than 10 usable areas, below the minimum of 10 the data test and the card use, so they stay out. Jaipur and Nagpur were read inside the municipal boundary. For the other cities (Ghaziabad, Varanasi, Meerut, Vadodara, Ludhiana, Nashik, Faridabad, Rajkot, and Aurangabad) OpenStreetMap had no municipal boundary at level 7 to 10 that contains the centre, so the search was inside the district boundary AND within 15 km of the city centre, which is an approximation of the city. Those areas are fewer and rougher than the ones taken from the Wikipedia categories.
- Headquarters: 21 of the 30 districts still have none, because the infobox has no value or the page does not exist: Zanskar, Sankoo, Changthang, Drass, Nubra and Sham (Ladakh); Longding, Bichom, Keyi Panyor (empty infobox) and Itanagar Capital Complex (no page) in Arunachal Pradesh; Khairagarh-Chhuikhadan-Gandai (Chhattisgarh); Malegaon (no page); Shahdara and Old Delhi (no value), North East Delhi, Central North Delhi and Outer North Delhi (Delhi: parts of one city, no page or only a locality); Chandigarh, Lakshadweep and Surguja are already in the file (their capitals are there).

## Known data issue for Opus to decide
Cities that were renamed are in the file under both names, because the two public lists use different names: Aurangabad and Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (both Maharashtra, same population 1,175,116), Osmanabad and Dharashiv. Ahilyanagar is in the file but its old name Ahmednagar is not. Importing both would give two pages for one city. The slugs `aurangabad` and `osmanabad` are the ones the OpenStreetMap areas use. A rename needs a decision on which name the site shows; it is not made here.

## Sources and rules followed
OpenStreetMap data is Open Database Licence; Wikipedia text is CC BY-SA. One request at a time, 20 seconds apart for the OpenStreetMap interpreter (a public server) and 8 seconds for Wikipedia, with a User-Agent that names the project and carries no personal details, and retries with a longer wait on any error.

## Tests
`tests/server/locations/h16-1-csv.test.ts` (the data checks of H16.1a: valid unique slugs, known states and cities, 10 to 40 areas per city, a source on every city row) and `h16-1-import.test.ts` (both built-in lists preview with no problems and import in seconds): 20 passed on the new files.
